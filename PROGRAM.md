# FORGE — 12-week program (the research behind it)

**Start point (Oct 2026):** 70 kg bodyweight, 175 cm. Weighted pull-up 1RM +40 kg, weighted dip 1RM +60 kg, a few freestanding HSPU, no 90° push-up.
**Past bests:** pull-up +70, dip +80, 90° HSPU for reps. Goal: get back there and go past it.

## The 7-day week
| Day | Session | Main work |
|---|---|---|
| D1 | Heavy Pull | Weighted pull-up (heavy), row, hanging leg raise, face pulls, run |
| D2 | Handstand Strength | Freestanding HS hold, freestanding HSPU, deficit wall HSPU, 90° negatives, run |
| D3 | Heavy Dip + Legs | Weighted dip (heavy), Romanian deadlift 3×5–8 @7, fast bodyweight pull-ups, L-sit, easy run |
| D4 | Recovery | Longest easy run, mobility, light handstand holds |
| D5 | Volume Pull + HSPU | Weighted pull-up (volume), wall HSPU, planche lean, face pulls, run |
| D6 | Volume Dip + 90° | Weighted dip (volume), 90° negatives, pseudo-planche push-ups, split squats, run + strides |
| D7 | Rest | Optional easy run, mobility |

You already did D1 (5×3 @ +30) and D2 (HSPU). **Your next session is W1·D3, Heavy Dip.**
The program goes in order, not by calendar date. If you miss a day you continue where you stopped, and nothing gets skipped.

## Blocks
| Weeks | Block | Heavy day | Volume day |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1–3 | Rebuild | 5×3 @ RPE 7 → 8 → 8.5 | 4×6 @ 7 → 8 |
| 4 | Deload | 1 single @8 + 2×3 @7 | 3×5 @6 |
| 5–7 | Strength | top single @8–9 + 3–4×2–3 back-offs | 4–5×4–5 @7.5–8 |
| 8 | Deload | 1 single @8 + 2×3 @7 | 3×4 @6 |
| 9–11 | Peak | top single @8.5–9 + doubles/singles @8.5–9 | 3–4×3–4 @7.5–8 |
| 12 | Test | Work up: single @8, @9, then a max attempt | light |

Runs: 20 min in weeks 1–2, 25 min in weeks 3–5, 30 min from week 6, always easy. D4 is +5 min. Strides on D6 from week 3.

## How the coach (autoregulation) works
1. **The max is estimated from every set.** e1RM = (bodyweight + added kg) ÷ %1RM, where %1RM comes from the reps and RPE you log (RTS/Tuchscherer chart). Bodyweight counts because you lift it too.
2. **Next session's loads** = e1RM × chart % for the prescribed reps and RPE, minus bodyweight, rounded to your plates.
3. **If you go over target** (RPE ≥1.5 above it, or you miss reps), the next session for that lift is one set shorter and RPE −0.5.
4. **Mid-workout:** if a set is ≥1 RPE off target, the remaining sets are recalculated, blended 50/50 with the plan so one bad guess can't swing it.
5. **A bad day only half-counts:** the estimate moves halfway toward a lower result. On a good day it can rise up to 4% per session.
6. **Check-in before each session:** if sleep, soreness and energy add up low, you get one less set and RPE −0.5.
7. **Bodyweight and skill exercises** use double progression. Hit every set at the target RPE and the target goes up. At the top of the range you level up (bigger deficit or harder variation) and the reps reset.

## Why it's built this way (evidence)
- **RPE/RIR autoregulation works at least as well as fixed percentages, and possibly better, for trained lifters.** In Helms et al. (2018), RPE loading did slightly better on 1RM, though the difference wasn't significant. In Graham & Cleather (2019), autoregulated training gained more (back squat +15.2 kg vs +9.1 kg). Lifters judge reps in reserve to within about 1 rep, and they're most accurate close to failure, so targets of RPE 7–9 are reliable.
- **Pull-ups and dips are calculated on total system load.** That's the standard way to compute weighted calisthenics 1RMs, and it's why the program never prescribes 5×3 at 91% (that's roughly RPE 10, which is how your D1 felt).
- **Muscle memory is real.** Previously trained muscle keeps extra myonuclei and an epigenetic "memory" (Seaborne et al. 2018; rodent myonuclei work). Expect a fast return toward +70/+80, faster than your first time building it. The engine allows up to 4% per session for this reason.
- **Running daily won't kill upper-body strength.** Recent meta-analyses (Schumann 2022, Huiberts 2023) found essentially no interference for upper-body maximal strength. What does show interference is hard lower-body work and sprinting near lifting. So: runs are easy, done after lifting or 3+ hours apart, and intervals are limited to light strides.
- **Linear and undulating periodization give similar strength results** (Harries et al. meta-analysis). Variety and progressive overload matter more, so the program mixes heavy and volume days and changes rep ranges every block.
- **HSPU and 90° progressions** (Barbell Physio, skill-tree resources): deficit wall HSPU, then freestanding singles and doubles, then 90° negatives with planche leans and pseudo-planche push-ups. Low reps for the hardest skills. Don't skip rungs.

## Legs
Two leg sessions a week: Romanian deadlift on D3 (hamstrings and glutes, the muscles that also protect you when running) and Bulgarian split squat on D6 (quads, one leg at a time). Both stay at RPE 7–8 so they don't clash with daily running. The run on D3 is kept truly easy.

## Rules that keep you healthy
- Leave reps in the tank. RPE 10 is for test week only.
- If an elbow or shoulder hurts (pain, not effort), swap that day's main lift for the volume version at RPE 6 and tell the app via the check-in.
- Sleep 7–9 h, eat around 1.6–2.2 g protein/kg, and keep a small surplus for the lean gain.
